Step 1 · Read

Cutting a long story into chapters

People have lived in the land we call Texas for more than 13,000 years. That is far too much to study all at once. So historians do what authors do with a long book: they split it into chapters. In history, those chapters are called eras.

An era is a stretch of time with defining characteristics, things that were true for most of that period. During the Spanish Colonial era, for example, Spain ruled Texas and built missions, forts called presidios, and ranches. When Mexico won its independence in 1821, the rules changed, so a new era began.

Eras help us see change over time. When you can name the era, you can ask good questions: Who was in charge? How did people make a living? What changed from the era before?

Historians disagree about edges. Did the Civil Rights era start in 1945 or 1954? Different books draw the lines differently. The dates below are approximate. What matters most is the big change that separates one era from the next.

The 12 eras of Texas history

Before 1519
Natural Texas and Its PeopleAmerican Indian groups such as the Caddo, Jumano, and Karankawa adapt to Texas's very different regions.
1519–1689
Age of ContactEuropean explorers like Álvarez de Pineda, Cabeza de Vaca, and La Salle reach Texas.
1690–1821
Spanish ColonialSpain builds missions, presidios, towns, and ranches to hold Texas.
1821–1835
Mexican NationalMexico rules Texas and invites settlers, including Stephen F. Austin's colonists.
1835–1845
Revolution and RepublicTexans fight for independence and run their own country, the Republic of Texas.
1845–1861
Early StatehoodTexas joins the United States and grows quickly.
1861–1874
Civil War and ReconstructionTexas secedes, fights for the Confederacy, and is rebuilt after the war.
1874–1900
Cotton, Cattle, and RailroadsCattle drives, cotton farming, and new railroads spread across the state.
1901–1929
Age of OilThe Spindletop gusher starts an oil boom that changes the economy.
1929–1945
Great Depression and World War IIHard times, the Dust Bowl, and a world war reshape Texas.
1945–1980
Civil Rights and ConservatismTexans fight for equal rights, and political loyalties begin to shift.
1980–today
Contemporary TexasBig cities, new technology, and fast population growth.

Eight dates every Texan should know

1519

Álvarez de Pineda maps the Texas coast; Spain founds its first settlement on the North American mainland.

1718

San Antonio is founded.

1821

Mexico, including Texas, wins independence from Spain.

1836

Texas declares independence from Mexico.

1845

Texas is annexed and becomes a U.S. state.

1861

The Civil War begins; Texas has seceded.

1876

Texans adopt the state constitution still used today.

1901

Oil gushes at Spindletop near Beaumont.

The historian's toolkit: two kinds of sources

Primary source

Made by someone who was there, at the time. Letters, diaries, photographs, laws, maps, and interviews with eyewitnesses are primary sources.

Example: William B. Travis's letter written inside the Alamo in 1836.

Secondary source

Made later by someone who studied the event but did not see it. Textbooks, encyclopedia articles, and most documentaries are secondary sources.

Example: an encyclopedia article about the Alamo written in 1990.

A quick test: Was the creator there when it happened? If yes, it is primary. If they learned about it afterward, it is secondary. Both are useful. Primary sources give you evidence; secondary sources help explain it.
