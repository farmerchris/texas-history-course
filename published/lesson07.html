The Texas Revolution

Big question: How did a small army win independence in seven weeks?

Step 1 · Read

From Gonzales to San Jacinto

In Lesson 6, Santa Anna threw out the Constitution of 1824 and peaceful requests failed. In the fall of 1835, the fighting began. At first, many Texians and Tejanos fought to restore the 1824 Constitution. By March 1836, they were fighting for independence.

The road to independence

Oct 2, 1835
GonzalesMexican soldiers came to take back a small cannon. Settlers flew a flag that said "Come and Take It" and drove them off. This was the first battle.
Dec 1835
Siege of BéxarTexian and Tejano volunteers, including Juan Seguín's company, captured San Antonio from Mexican troops.
Feb 23 – Mar 6, 1836
The AlamoSanta Anna's army surrounded about 180 to 250 defenders in the old mission. On March 6 the Mexican army stormed the walls. Nearly all the defenders died, including William B. Travis, James Bowie, and David Crockett.
Mar 2, 1836
Convention of 1836Delegates at Washington-on-the-Brazos signed the Texas Declaration of Independence. George Childress was its main author. Lorenzo de Zavala, a Mexican federalist leader, signed it and became interim vice president. Sam Houston was named commander of the army.
Mar 27, 1836
GoliadColonel James Fannin surrendered his men after a battle near Goliad. On Santa Anna's orders, about 340 prisoners were executed. "Remember Goliad!" became a battle cry.
Apr 21, 1836
San JacintoHouston's army of about 900 attacked Santa Anna's camp in the afternoon while many soldiers rested. The battle lasted about 18 minutes. Santa Anna was captured the next day and agreed to order his army out of Texas.
1836Texas declares independence

Read the source: Travis writes from the Alamo

William B. Travis, February 24, 1836 · excerpt

“To the People of Texas & All Americans in the World—

Fellow Citizens & compatriots—I am besieged, by a thousand or more of the Mexicans under Santa Anna. … The enemy has demanded a surrender at discretion, otherwise, the garrison are to be put to the sword, if the fort is taken—I have answered the demand with a cannon shot, & our flag still waves proudly from the walls—I shall never surrender or retreat.

Then, I call on you in the name of Liberty, of patriotism & everything dear to the American character, to come to our aid, with all dispatch. … Victory or Death.”

Annotate as you read: Who is his audience? What is his purpose? What words does he choose to persuade them?

The defenders were a mix of people

The Alamo defenders came from many places. Most were recent arrivals from the United States, but some were born in England, Ireland, Scotland, Germany, and Denmark. Tejanos fought inside the walls too, such as Gregorio Esparza and José Toribio Losoya. Some survivors were women and children, like Susanna Dickinson and Esparza's family, and Joe, a man Travis held in slavery. Juan Seguín survived because Travis sent him out as a messenger to find help.

Key people

Sam Houstoncommander

Retreated east for weeks while his army trained and grew, then struck at San Jacinto.

Santa AnnaMexico's president

Led the Mexican army. His orders of no mercy at the Alamo and Goliad angered Texians and brought in volunteers.

Juan SeguínTejano captain

Messenger from the Alamo. His company of Tejanos fought at San Jacinto.

Lorenzo de Zavalasigner

Helped write Mexico's 1824 constitution, then signed Texas's Declaration of Independence.

How did a small army win? The Alamo and Goliad cost lives but bought time and turned anger into recruits. Houston's long retreat, called the Runaway Scrape for the families who fled with it, kept his army alive. Santa Anna split his forces and let his guard down. When Houston finally attacked, surprise did the rest. Independence was declared on March 2 and won on April 21: seven weeks.
