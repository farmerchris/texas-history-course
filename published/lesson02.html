Step 1 · Read

One state, four very different lands

In Lesson 1 you met the first era of Texas history: Natural Texas and Its People, the thousands of years before Europeans arrived. To understand the people of that era, start with the land itself.

Texas is huge, and its land changes a lot from one side to the other. Geographers split it into four physical regions. A region is an area where the land, climate, and plants are alike. The biggest difference between the regions is rain. East Texas gets a lot of it. Far West Texas gets very little.

Rain decides what grows. What grows decides what animals live there. And plants and animals decided how people could feed, house, and clothe themselves.

The four physical regions

Four ways of life

Historians group the American Indian peoples of early Texas into four culture groups. People in the same culture group got food, built homes, and moved (or stayed put) in similar ways, because they lived on similar land.

The geographer's toolkit: adapt or modify?

Adapt

Change the way you live to fit the land. Following the bison herds is adapting.

Example: Plains peoples made tipis they could pack up and carry.

Modify

Change the land to fit what you need. Clearing trees to plant a field is modifying.

Example: Caddo farmers cleared forest to grow corn.

A quick test: Who changed, the people or the land? If the people changed their habits, they adapted. If they changed the land, they modified it. Every group did some of both.

How do we know? Most of these groups did not leave written records. Archaeologists learn about them from artifacts such as tools, pottery, and the remains of houses. Spanish and French explorers also wrote about them, but those writers were outsiders. Remember Lesson 1: a primary source shows the creator's point of view.
These stories did not end. Many of these nations are still here. The Caddo Nation, Comanche Nation, and Wichita and Affiliated Tribes are based in Oklahoma today. In Texas, the Tigua people of Ysleta del Sur Pueblo live near El Paso, along with two other tribes, the Alabama-Coushatta and the Kickapoo.
