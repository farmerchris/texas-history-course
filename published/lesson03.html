Step 1 · Read

Strangers on the shore

In Lesson 2 you met the peoples who had lived in Texas for thousands of years. In 1519, something new appeared off the coast: European ships. Historians call the next period the Age of Contact, the time when American Indians and Europeans first met.

Why did Europeans come at all? Teachers often sum it up as gold, God, and glory.

GoldWealth

Spain had found silver and gold in Mexico. Explorers hoped Texas held more. They also hunted for a water route to Asia and its spices.

GodReligion

Spain and France were Catholic countries. Priests traveled with explorers to spread their faith to the people they met.

GloryPower and fame

Kings wanted land before rival countries grabbed it. Explorers wanted fame, titles, and rewards for their service.

Who came, and what they found

1519
Alonso Álvarez de PinedaA Spanish captain who sailed the whole Gulf coast looking for a water route to Asia. He found no route, but he drew the first map of the Texas coast.
1528
Cabeza de Vaca and EstevanicoPart of a Spanish expedition that fell apart. Their rafts washed ashore near Galveston Island. Only a few men survived. They lived among the Karankawa and other peoples for years as captives, traders, and healers.
1536
Four survivors reach MexicoCabeza de Vaca, Estevanico, and two other Spaniards walked across Texas and northern Mexico until they found Spanish soldiers. They told stories of rich cities farther north.
1541
Francisco Vázquez de CoronadoLed a large army searching for the Seven Cities of Gold. He crossed the Texas Panhandle and found grasslands, huge bison herds, and villages of farmers. He found no gold.
1685
René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La SalleA French explorer who planned a colony at the mouth of the Mississippi River. He missed it and landed at Matagorda Bay, where he built Fort Saint Louis. Hunger, disease, and conflict ruined the colony. His own men killed him in 1687.
1689
Alonso de LeónA Spanish governor sent to find the French. He found the fort in ruins. Spain now feared France would take Texas, which set up Lesson 4.
What did they find? No gold and no shortcut to Asia. They found nations of people with their own leaders and trade, open grasslands full of bison, and a coast that wrecked ships. After Coronado, Spain mostly ignored Texas for about 140 years. It took a French colony to bring Spain back.
Estevanico

He was an enslaved man from Azemmour, Morocco. He is the first African known to have crossed Texas. He was skilled at learning languages and became the group's go-between. He left no writing of his own, so we only know him through what others wrote.

La Belle

La Salle's ship La Belle sank in Matagorda Bay in 1686. In 1995 Texas Historical Commission archaeologists found it, pumped the water out from around it, and dug it up. Its hull is now on display at the Bullock Museum in Austin.

Contact had a cost. Europeans carried diseases such as smallpox and measles that American Indians had never faced. Their bodies had no defense against them. Over the next two centuries, these diseases killed a huge share of the Native people of Texas, often before many of them had ever met a European.

Reading a primary source: whose eyes?

Cabeza de Vaca wrote about his journey in a report published in 1542. It is one of the oldest written descriptions of Texas and its people. It is also a source with a strong point of view.

Ask three questions about any source: Who wrote it? Who was it for? Why? Cabeza de Vaca wrote for the king of Spain. He had lost the expedition and wanted to prove he had still served God and the king well. He hoped for a reward, and he got one: a job as a governor in South America.

That does not make him a liar. It means some of what he wrote is something he saw, and some is how he interpreted what he saw. Your job in Practice B is to tell the difference.
