Reform, Depression & the World Wars

Big question: How did national crises reshape Texas from 1900 to 1945?

About 90 minutesTEKS 7.7C, 7.7E, 7.9B, 7.17B, 7.18DYour work saves on this device

Step 1 · Read

Four big shocks in 45 years

Between 1900 and 1945, the whole nation faced reform movements, a world war, a deep depression, and a second world war. Each one reached into Texas and changed its politics, its economy, and daily life. As you read, tag each change as political (government and voting), economic (jobs and money), or social (how people live together).

Reform

Populists1890s

Farmers angry at railroads and banks formed the People's Party. They lost most elections, but many of their ideas, like regulating big business, were later adopted.

Progressives1900s–1910s

Reformers wanted cleaner, more efficient government. After the 1900 storm (Lesson 12), Galveston invented the city commission form of government, which spread nationwide.

Woman suffrage1918–19

Leaders like Minnie Fisher Cunningham and Jane Y. McCallum won Texas women the right to vote in primaries in 1918. In 1919, Texas became the first Southern state to approve the 19th Amendment.

The wars and the Depression

1917–18
World War INearly 200,000 Texans served. Army camps and airfields opened across Texas, including Kelly Field in San Antonio, because of the mild weather and open land.
1929
The Great Depression beginsPrices for cotton and oil collapsed. Farmers lost their land and workers lost jobs.
1930s
The Dust BowlYears of drought and over-plowing on the southern plains let wind strip away topsoil. Huge dust storms rolled over the Panhandle, burying fences and ruining crops.
1933–39
The New DealFederal programs put people to work. The Civilian Conservation Corps built Texas state parks, such as Palo Duro Canyon and Bastrop. Texans in Washington helped lead it: Vice President John Nance Garner of Uvalde and Congressman Sam Rayburn, who later became the longest-serving Speaker of the House and pushed to bring electricity to rural Texas.
1941–45
World War IIAbout 750,000 Texans served, including about 12,000 women. Admiral Chester Nimitz of Fredericksburg commanded the Pacific Fleet. Doris Miller of Waco was honored for heroism at Pearl Harbor. Oveta Culp Hobby led the Women's Army Corps. Bases, shipyards, and factories boomed, pulling Texans from farms into cities.
Why do these crises matter for Texas? They pushed Texas from a farm state toward a city and factory state. By 1950, most Texans lived in cities for the first time. They also tied Texas more closely to the national government, which now built parks, paid farmers, and set up military bases across the state.

Texas voices of the era

J. Frank Dobiewriter

Collected and retold Texas folk tales about longhorns, mustangs, and vaqueros.

Walter Prescott Webbhistorian

Wrote The Great Plains, showing how the dry land shaped the people who lived on it.

Elisabet Neysculptor

Carved statues of Sam Houston and Stephen F. Austin that stand in the Texas and U.S. Capitols.

Scott Joplincomposer

Grew up in Texarkana and became the "King of Ragtime."
