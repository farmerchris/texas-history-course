Step 1 · Read

Holding a huge land with very few people

At the end of Lesson 3, Spain found La Salle's fort in ruins. The French were gone, but the scare worked. Spain decided it had to hold Texas before France or anyone else did.

There was a problem. Very few Spaniards wanted to move to a far-off frontier with no gold. So Spain tried a different plan. It built four kinds of places, and it hoped to turn the American Indians who already lived there into Catholic, Spanish-speaking subjects who would help hold the land.

How the plan played out

1682
Ysleta, near El PasoThe first mission in what is now Texas, built for Tigua people who fled south after a revolt in New Mexico. Their descendants still live there.
1690
San Francisco de los TejasFather Damián Massanet and Alonso de León built a mission among the Caddo in East Texas. Disease and conflict drove the friars out by 1693.
1716
Back to East TexasFrance was close again, in Louisiana. Spain returned with several missions and a presidio. Father Antonio Margil de Jesús founded some of them.
1718
San Antonio is foundedA mission and a presidio went up beside the San Antonio River, halfway between Mexico and East Texas. It became the heart of Spanish Texas.
1731
Canary Islanders arriveAbout 16 families from the Canary Islands founded San Fernando de Béxar, the first town in Texas with its own town government.
1772
East Texas abandonedSpain now owned Louisiana, so the French threat was gone. Spain pulled settlers out of East Texas and made San Antonio the capital.

A mission looked different depending on who you were. The friars believed they were saving souls. For some Native families, such as the Coahuiltecans, a mission offered food in hard times and protection from Apache and Comanche raids. It also meant hard work, strict rules, giving up old beliefs, and deadly disease spreading through crowded rooms. Many people left, and some came back. Both views belong in the story.
Why the plan struggled. Spanish Texas never had many settlers. By 1800 only a few thousand lived there. The Comanche and Apache were strong, and the Comanche controlled much of the plains. Supplies came from far away in Mexico. Spain held San Antonio, Goliad, and Nacogdoches, but most of Texas stayed out of its reach.

What lasted

Spain ruled Texas for over a hundred years. Look around Texas today and you can still see its mark.

Wordslanguage

Rodeo, corral, lasso, ranch, mustang, and bronco all come from Spanish.

Place namesmaps

San Antonio, El Paso, Amarillo (yellow), the Brazos, the Nueces, and the Rio Grande.

Ranchingwork

Vaqueros taught later cowboys how to rope and herd from horseback. Longhorn cattle descend from Spanish cattle.

Buildingsarchitecture

The San Antonio missions are a World Heritage Site, the only one in Texas, named in 2015.
