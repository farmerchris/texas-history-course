From Spanish Texas to Mexican Texas

At the end of Lesson 4, Spain still held San Antonio, Goliad, and Nacogdoches, but it never had enough settlers. Then a war in Mexico changed everything.

In 1810, a priest named Miguel Hidalgo rang the church bell in the town of Dolores and called on the people of Mexico to rise up against Spain. That fight for independence lasted eleven years, and some of it spilled into Texas.

Why invite settlers?

Mexico had the same problem Spain had: a huge land with few people. Comanche and Apache raids made the frontier dangerous, and Mexico worried the United States wanted Texas. Mexican leaders hoped loyal, hardworking settlers would farm the land, pay taxes, and act as a buffer against raids and against the United States.

The empresarios

Stephen F. Austinempresario

Brought the first 300 families, called the Old Three Hundred, to the Brazos and Colorado river valleys. He learned Spanish, worked closely with Mexican officials, and is called the Father of Texas.

Martín De Leónempresario

Founded the town of Victoria in 1824. His was the only colony settled mostly by Mexican families.

Erasmo SeguínTejano leader

A San Antonio leader who welcomed the Austins, represented Texas at the meeting that wrote the Constitution of 1824, and pushed for laws that opened Texas to settlers.

Slavery and the plan. Many Anglo settlers came from the southern United States to grow cotton, and some brought enslaved African Americans to do the work. Mexico was moving to end slavery, and in 1829 its president abolished it. Texas settlers won an exemption, and some got around the rules by calling enslaved people "indentured servants." This argument over slavery became one more source of conflict between settlers and the government.
Why the plan backfired. The plan worked too well. By 1830, settlers from the United States far outnumbered Tejanos. Most lived apart from Mexican towns, spoke English, traded with the U.S., and did not follow Mexican customs or laws closely. Mexico had invited people to protect Texas, and now it worried it might lose Texas to them. That worry is where Lesson 6 begins.
