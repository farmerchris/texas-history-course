Step 1 · Read

Unfinished business

In Lesson 10, freed Texans won rights during Reconstruction, then lost many of them. By 1900, Texas laws kept Black and white Texans apart in schools, trains, and parks, a system called segregation. Black Texans were blocked from voting in Democratic primaries, which in one-party Texas decided most elections. Mexican Americans faced segregated "Mexican schools," juries that left them out, and signs that said they were not welcome.

Different groups fought back in different ways: in courtrooms, at the ballot box, and in the streets. As you read, compare their goals and their strategies.

Leaders and organizations

LULACfounded 1929

The League of United Latin American Citizens began in Corpus Christi. It worked through courts and education, backing lawsuits against school segregation such as Delgado v. Bastrop ISD (1948), and Hernandez v. Texas (1954), where the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that Mexican Americans could not be kept off juries.

Hector P. GarcíaAmerican GI Forum

A doctor and World War II veteran in Corpus Christi. In 1948 he founded the American GI Forum to fight for Mexican American veterans. In 1949 a funeral home in Three Rivers refused a chapel to the family of Felix Longoria, a soldier killed in the war. García's protest led to Longoria's burial at Arlington National Cemetery.

Lulu B. WhiteNAACP, Houston

Led the Houston branch of the NAACP in the 1940s and built it into one of the largest in the South. She supported the cases that ended the white primary (Smith v. Allwright, 1944) and opened the University of Texas law school to Heman Sweatt (Sweatt v. Painter, 1950).

James FarmerCORE

Born in Marshall and a star debater at Wiley College. He co-founded the Congress of Racial Equality (CORE), which used nonviolent protest, and he organized the 1961 Freedom Rides to challenge segregated buses in the South.

Barbara Jordanlawmaker

In 1966 she became the first African American elected to the Texas Senate since 1883. In 1972 she became the first Black woman from the South elected to the U.S. House, where her speech on the Constitution during the Watergate hearings made her nationally known.

Political change: Texas turns Republican

For about 100 years after Reconstruction, almost every Texas officeholder was a Democrat. Starting in the 1960s, a conservative movement built the Republican Party in Texas.

1961
John TowerWon a U.S. Senate seat, the first Republican elected to statewide office in Texas since Reconstruction.
1978
Bill ClementsElected the first Republican governor since Reconstruction.
1980s–90s
RealignmentMany conservative Democrats switched parties. Texans voted for Republicans for president and more state offices.
