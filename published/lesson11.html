Step 1 · Read

Texas after the war

When Reconstruction ended (Lesson 10), Texas was poor but full of two things: land and cattle. Over the next 30 years, cattle drives, railroads, and the end of the open frontier changed where Texans lived and how they made a living. That change came at a terrible cost for American Indians.

The cattle drives

During the war, millions of longhorns ran wild in South Texas. A steer worth a few dollars in Texas could sell for many times more in the North, where cities wanted beef. So from 1866 to the 1880s, cowboys drove herds north to railroad towns in Kansas, where cattle were loaded onto trains.

Chisholm Trailto Abilene, KS

The most famous route, running north from South Texas through Fort Worth.

Western Trailto Dodge City, KS

Ran farther west as farms and fences filled in the land along the Chisholm.

Goodnight-Loving Trailto New Mexico

Charles Goodnight and Oliver Loving drove herds west and north to forts and mining towns.

Who were the cowboys? Many were young men back from the war. Many were African American or Mexican American, and Mexican vaqueros (Lesson 4) had taught the skills they all used. By the late 1880s, barbed wire fences, windmills, and railroads reaching into Texas itself ended the long drives.

Railroads remake the map

In 1870, Texas had only a few hundred miles of railroad track. By 1900 it had nearly 10,000 miles. Railroads let farmers ship cotton and ranchers ship cattle from anywhere along the line, so new towns grew up beside the tracks. Towns the railroad skipped often withered. Railroads also brought settlers to West Texas and the Panhandle.

The end of the open frontier

The bison1870s

Hide hunters killed millions of bison on the Southern Plains in just a few years. The Comanche, Kiowa, and other Plains nations depended on the bison for food, shelter, and tools (Lesson 2).

Buffalo soldiersU.S. Army

African American regiments served at frontier forts such as Fort Concho and Fort Davis. They guarded roads, mapped West Texas, and fought in campaigns against American Indians.

Red River War1874–75

The U.S. Army attacked Comanche, Kiowa, and Cheyenne camps. At Palo Duro Canyon, soldiers destroyed their winter food and killed more than 1,000 horses.

Quanah ParkerComanche leader

The son of a Comanche chief and Cynthia Ann Parker, a captured settler. He led his band onto a reservation in Oklahoma in 1875, then became a respected leader and rancher who worked to protect his people's land and rights.

Farmers fight back

Cotton was still king, but many farmers, white and Black, worked land they did not own as tenant farmers or sharecroppers. They complained that railroads charged unfair rates to ship their crops. Farmers organized groups like the Grange and the Farmers' Alliance. In 1890, James Stephen Hogg was elected governor, the first born in Texas. He created the Texas Railroad Commission in 1891 to regulate railroad rates.

Reading a map pattern (SE 21B). When you compare an 1870 railroad map with a 1900 map, ask: Where are the new lines? Which towns appear along them? What does that tell you about where Texans lived and worked? A pattern is something that repeats across the map, like "new towns appear along the tracks."
