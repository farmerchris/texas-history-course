Step 1 · Read

Your turn to be the historian

All year you have read sources, made claims, and backed them with evidence. Now you will choose your own topic from any era of Texas history, research it, and show what you found as a poster, a short video, or a 2-page paper. This page gets you started: you'll practice the research skills, then write a project proposal for a parent to approve.

The project steps

Step 1
Choose a topicPick one person, place, or event. Make it narrow enough to explain well. "The Texas Revolution" is too big; "Juan Seguín's role in the Revolution" is just right.
Step 2
Find sourcesYou need at least one primary source and two secondary sources. Start with the Handbook of Texas Online and the Portal to Texas History.
Step 3
Take notes and citeWrite down where every fact came from as you go. It is much harder to find later.
Step 4
Make a claimAnswer the big question for your topic: how did it change Texas? Your claim is the center of the project.
Step 5
Build and presentMake the poster, video, or paper. Organize it around your claim and evidence, and include a source list.

Where to look

Secondary sources

Written later by people who studied the past: Handbook of Texas Online, Bullock Texas State History Museum, library books.

Primary sources

Made at the time by people who were there: letters, photos, maps, and newspapers on the Portal to Texas History, documents at the Texas State Library and Archives.

How to cite a source

A simple citation format

Author (if known). “Title of the page or article.” Name of the website or book, date published. Web address.

Example: Wooster, Robert, and Christine Moor Sanders. “Spindletop Oilfield.” Handbook of Texas Online, Texas State Historical Association. https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/spindletop-oilfield

How your project will be graded

Category4 points means…
Accurate historyFacts, names, and dates are correct and explained in your own words.
SourcesAt least one primary and two secondary sources, each cited correctly.
ClaimA clear claim about how your topic changed Texas, backed by evidence.
PresentationClear, organized, and easy to follow, with your claim up front.
